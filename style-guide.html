Style guide

How the city writes.

Five tests Voice Labels For everyone Numbers Spelling Languages Sources Apple

Five tests

Every line passes all five before it goes live.

Clear
A first-time reader understands it without asking. If someone has to ask what it means, rewrite it.
True
Every sentence is true of the thing it describes, checked against the page, the code or the source.
Short
As few words as the idea needs. Most good edits are cuts.
Said once
One idea, one line, one place. A lab’s gray line is also its intro and its description, word for word.
Ready for four languages
Plain sentences and no idioms, so English, 简体, 繁體 and 日本語 can all say the same thing.

Voice

The city speaks as “we”: warm, plain and sure of itself.

“We”, not “I”
Lines, stations, labs and pages say “we” and “our”. Articles keep their author’s own voice; we fix only typos and formatting.
Plain, not clever
No jargon, no wordplay, no exclamation points. Leave out “simply” and “just”: they tell readers something is easy.
True, not sweeping
Claim what holds and no more: “add value to others with peace of mind”, not “anything that adds value”.
Their own words
When the author has already said it well, use those words: “Action precedes motivation”, 知行合一.
Easy first
The main thing first; detail when the reader asks for it.
Nothing that dates
No counts or “so far” that go stale. Describe what is there.

Labels

The short words that tell you where a link goes and what a button does.

Gray lines
Led by a verb, “Plan the city behind the map”, or a noun with a brief intro, “Marathons around the world”.
Sentence case
“Metro design system”, not “Metro Design System”. Names keep their capitals: City Hall, the Kaizen line.
Headings
Plain names a reader understands at once: “The network and its map”, not “The network as a diagram”.
Buttons
Say what happens: “Play the chime”, “Hear an arrival at Central”.
Symbols
No emoji. A symbol a phone could draw as one, like ▶︎ or ✈︎, carries the text-style marker.

For everyone

Words that include everyone, in every language.

They
When we don’t know someone’s pronouns, “they”. A name doesn’t tell you.
What happens
Describe what happens, not a sense: “the times count down”, not “watch the times”.
Color is a line
Never use a color to mean good or bad. On our map, color means a line.
No idioms
Sayings and wordplay don’t translate. Write plain sentences.
Words with a past
Check where a word comes from. Leave out violent or oppressive terms, like “kill” or “master and slave”.
A study’s own terms
Describe a study in its own words, such as “healthy adults”. Elsewhere, don’t call people “normal” or “healthy” to mean without a disability.

Numbers

Dates, times and measures, written one way everywhere.

Dates
Month, day, year: November 13, 2026, with a comma after the year when the sentence goes on.
Times
The 24-hour clock: 08:00.
Ranges
An en dash with no spaces: 1908–1933, 4–6 h.
Units
A space between number and unit: 250 km, 16 px, 400 mg.
Thousands
A comma: 1,600.
Small numbers
One to nine in words in a sentence, “every five meters”; numerals for 10 and up, and in ranges, tables and data.

Spelling

American spelling, light punctuation.

American
Color, center, meter, organizer. Names, quotes and titles keep their own spelling: Harbour station, the International Labour Organization.
Lists
No comma before the last “and”, unless the sense needs one: rigor, taste, resonance and discipline.
Quotation marks
Curly in text: “ ” and ’.
Dashes
A colon or a comma where either will do; an en dash for ranges.

Languages

English, 简体, 繁體 and 日本語 change together.

Together
When one changes, all four change. Quotes and photo captions first written in English stay in English.
Meaning, not words
Translate what it means, then read it aloud: 我们服务的人, not 为之服务的人.
Names first
On a page that is still changing, translate the names beside the English, and the rest when it settles.

Sources

Every figure comes from somewhere you can check.

Checked, then cited
Every figure in a lab and every fact in Around the world links the page it came from, checked against that page.
How sure
Say how sure a figure is, as the labs’ labels do.
Photos
Freely licensed, credited under the photo, and described as they are.
Or leave it out
If it can’t be sourced, it doesn’t go in.

Apple

What we took from the Apple Style Guide (June 2026), and where we differ on purpose.

One voice
The same voice in every kind of text, from a button to a long page.
Write for everyone
“They” for unknown pronouns, no idioms, no colors for good or bad, and what happens rather than what you see or hear.
International style
Simple sentences, few abbreviations, the 24-hour clock and a space before units.
Words with a past
Research a word’s history before using it.
