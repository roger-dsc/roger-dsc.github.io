To find your own sweat rate, weigh yourself before and after a run: every kilogram lost is about a liter of sweat, plus whatever you drank?.

Your weight by the finish Guideline

Too little Guideline

The American College of Sports Medicine’s goal for drinking during exercise is to prevent excessive (>2% body weight loss from water deficit) dehydration. Sweat rates and the salt in sweat vary a lot between people, so it advises plans fitted to each person?.

Too much Large study

At the 2002 Boston Marathon, 13% of 488 runners finished with low blood sodium (hyponatremia), and 0.6% critically low. Gaining weight during the race was the strongest risk (odds ratio 4.2), then a time over 4 hours and a very low or high BMI?. Don’t drink more than you sweat.

Your weeks

Fitness, fatigue and form Early evidence

Mostly easy Large study

Studies of national and international endurance athletes converge on about 80% of sessions at low intensity and about 20% hard, such as intervals at about 90% of VO₂max?. In a 9-week trial of 48 well-trained athletes, this “polarised” mix raised VO₂peak the most: by 11.7%?.

The taper Trials pooled

Across 27 studies, the best taper lasted two weeks, with training volume cut by 41–60%?.

Strength work Trials pooled

In 5 controlled trials of 93 high-level middle- and long-distance runners, strength and jump training 2–3 times a week for 8–12 weeks made running clearly more economical (standardized mean difference −1.42)?.

“Add no more than 10% a week” Did not hold up

In a trial of 532 novice runners, a 13-week plan built on the 10% rule had as many injuries as a standard 8-week plan: 20.8% against 20.3%?. In 874 novices with GPS watches, those who added more than 30% over two weeks had more overuse injuries of the kind that come from distance (hazard ratio 1.59, not statistically certain)?.
